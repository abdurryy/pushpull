Push/Pull paper test kit

Everything needed to run user test 1 on paper: the facilitator's script, a note sheet, the board, four controllers, direction cards and tokens. The plan for the session is in issue #31.

Printing: print all 7 pages on A4, landscape, at 100% scale (not "fit to page", or the board squares will not match the tokens). Black and white is fine. You need one copy of each page, except the controller pages, which give you the four controllers, and the note sheet, which is worth printing twice.

This box does not print.

Facilitator script

User test 1, paper prototype. Keep this page in front of you. 30 minutes.

Before they arrive

  • Board on the table, both snakes on their start squares.
  • One controller and four direction cards per player.
  • Note sheet and a pen for the note-taker. Pencil and eraser for the board.
  • Roles: one person talks, one writes, one moves the snakes. Two people can do it if the facilitator also moves the snakes.

0 to 3 min: welcome

"Thanks for helping. We are testing a party game we are building, not you. Nothing here can go wrong. Please say out loud what you are thinking while you play, even when it is confusing. Is it OK if we write notes? We will not use your name. May we take photos of the table, with no faces in them?"

3 to 6 min: set up

Two teams of two. Teammates sit next to each other. Hand out the controllers.

"You are two teams. Each team has one snake. Both of you steer the same snake, at the same time. Eat apples to grow. Do not hit a wall or a snake. Every few seconds I will say 'three, two, one, show', and you both put a direction card on your controller at the same time. Then I move the snakes."

Do not explain what happens when teammates pick different directions. Whether they work that out is the main thing we want to learn.

6 to 21 min: three rounds

A round is 20 moves, or until a snake crashes. Call "three, two, one, show" about every 5 seconds.

  1. Round 1. "Get your snake to the apple before the other team."
  2. Between rounds. "Make your snake stop where it is."
  3. Round 2. "Turn your snake around and bring it back to where you started."
  4. Round 3. "Grow as long as you can and still be alive on the last move."

Do not help unless they are completely stuck. If you do help, the note-taker writes it down. The next page tells whoever moves the snakes what to do on each move.

21 to 28 min: questions

  1. What happened when you and your teammate pointed different ways?
  2. Could you tell whether your card counted? How?
  3. How did you and your teammate decide where to go?
  4. Was there a moment you did not know what to do? What did you do then?
  5. What was the most fun moment? The most annoying one?
  6. These four buttons are on the controller but we have not built them yet. What would you expect each one to do?
  7. From 1 to 5, how much would you want to play another round?

28 to 30 min: close

"Thank you. Is there anything else you want to tell us?"

Write down the real start and end time. The session has to be at least 30 minutes.

Moving the snakes

For whoever plays the computer. These are the rules the built game follows, so the paper game behaves the same way.

Every move

  1. Say "three, two, one, show". Both teammates put a card down at the same time.
  2. Work out each team's direction with the table below.
  3. Move each head one square and shade it with the pencil.
  4. Erase the last square of the tail, unless the snake just ate the apple. An apple makes it one square longer, so leave the tail alone that move.
  5. If a head lands on the apple, put the apple token on another free square.
  6. If a head lands on the Lucky Box, draw a card, read it out and do what it says.

What the team's two cards mean

The two cardsThe snake
The same directionGoes that way
Opposite directionsDoes not move this turn
Different, not opposite, and one is the way it is already goingKeeps going that way
Different, not opposite, and neither is the way it is already goingFollows player 1's card
Only one card downFollows that card

When a round ends

  • After 20 moves, or
  • when a head hits a wall, its own body or the other snake.

The longer snake wins the round. Set the board back up and start the next one.

Start of a round

Team A on C7, D7 and E7, head on E7 facing right. Team B on P7, Q7 and R7, head on P7 facing left. Put the apple on a free square, and the Lucky Box on another one.

If you are asked

Say what happened, not why: "the snake did not move". Do not explain the rule about opposite directions. We want to see whether they work it out.

Lucky Box: draw a card when a snake reaches the box

Growth spurtYour snake grows by 3 squares.
ShrinkYour snake loses 2 squares from its tail.
Length heistTake 2 squares from the other team and add them to your snake.
Tail snipThe other team loses 2 squares from its tail.
Length swapThe two snakes swap lengths.
Triple biteThe next apple you eat grows you by 3 instead of 1.
FreezeThe other team misses its next move.
Apple deliveryMove the apple to a free square next to your head.

These eight outcomes are the ones in the built game. On paper, one square stands for one length.
